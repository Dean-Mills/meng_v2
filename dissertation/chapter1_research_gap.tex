\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec_intro_gap}

The grouping mechanisms of established bottom-up methods are tightly coupled to their host detectors.
Part-affinity fields~\cite{cao2017openpose, kreiss2019pifpaf} share a backbone with the heatmap head;
associative-embedding tags~\cite{newell2017associative, cheng2020higherhrnet}
are calibrated to the feature scale of the network they are trained with;
centre-offset methods~\cite{geng2021dekr, braso2021centergroup} fold grouping into a backbone-specific regression head;
and end-to-end set-prediction methods~\cite{shi2022petr, yang2023edpose}
never expose a per-keypoint representation at all.
In all these cases, swapping the detector after training is not supported.

A \emph{modular} grouping module is one that takes detected keypoints from any 
detector, runs a separable algorithm and returns per-keypoint person identities. Such modules are fairly unstudied.
The closest graph-based precedents are HGG~\cite{jin2020hgg} and GEC~\cite{maher2025gec}.
HGG is trained jointly with its detector and is therefore not modular;
GEC is detector-independent but uses a per-edge binary classification rather than a per-keypoint embedding.
No prior work, in this modular setting, trains a detector-independent 
graph-neural-network embedding for multi-person 
keypoint grouping, or addresses autonomous person-count estimation as part of the same pipeline.
This is the research gap the present work addresses; the supporting literature is reviewed in full in Chapter~\ref{chap_2}.
